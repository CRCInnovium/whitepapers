## Abstract

Agentic research often asks what an agent can do on a benchmark. Institutions face a different question: what does it take for hundreds of scientists across disciplines to adopt autonomous agents in their daily work and continue using them? This research addresses that question through an institutional deployment.

InnoviumAI is a multi-agent orchestration platform developed at the Technology Innovation Institute (TII) in Abu Dhabi. It gives researchers a shared environment for supervised agent teams connected to their scientific tools. We study how adoption spreads, whether use is sustained, and how four design decisions address barriers to institutional deployment. A desktop application bundles its runtime to simplify installation. Centralised provider access removes the need for researchers to manage shared model credentials. Human approval and recorded actions support oversight. A versioned marketplace lets teams share and adapt agents, tools and workflows while preserving local customisations.

By 15 September 2026, the platform had 433 registered accounts, with 305 recording model activity. An executive report documents 18 use cases across seven research centres, spanning scientific reporting, materials research, energy modelling and engineering simulation. Centre-reported gains include gas-turbine reporting reduced from three hours to five minutes. Applications also extend to an AI-assisted heat-exchanger design that progressed through simulation, manufacturing and laboratory testing.

The proposed study combines longitudinal adoption analysis, evaluation of the four design choices, and cross-team workflow reuse. It aims to produce institutional evidence and a tested reference architecture, supporting wider adoption at TII and a practical pathway to UAE commercialisation and government replication.
